Overview

A self-directed analytical investigation into Nigeria's agricultural production and food security landscape, built as an end-to-end demonstration of the analytical workflow: data sourcing through data auditing, cleaning, modelling, SQL analysis, and dashboard visualization.

Complete

Problem

The project examines a structural contradiction in Nigeria's food system: national crop production increased substantially while welfare indicators deteriorated. The analysis tests whether food security can be understood from production alone or whether access, prices, trade exposure, and welfare conditions must be considered together.

Complete

Research Question

  • What are the regional contributions to food security in Nigeria?
  • What structural factors drive food-security outcomes across production, trade, and welfare dimensions?
  • How does agricultural production vary by region and crop?
  • How has agricultural production changed over time?
  • What role does import dependency play in food-system resilience and access?
Complete

Objectives

The project investigates regional food-security contributions in Nigeria and examines how production, trade, prices, and welfare indicators interact. It integrates survey, production, trade, price, and macroeconomic data in a single analytical workflow and evaluates the relationship between supply expansion and food-security outcomes.

Complete

Why It Matters

The project finds that aggregate national crop production increased while undernourishment also rose. This indicates that food security cannot be assessed from production or caloric availability alone; access, prices, trade exposure, distribution, and welfare conditions require equal attention.

Complete

Data

The project combines five major data domains: Nigeria LSMS Wave 4 household and agricultural survey data; FAOSTAT crop production and food-balance data; UN Comtrade bilateral trade data; Nigeria retail food-price data; and World Bank World Development Indicators. The resulting analytical database contains 15 documented tables, including dimensions, fact tables, and staging tables.

Complete

Data Sources

The project uses source data from Nigeria LSMS Wave 4, FAOSTAT, UN Comtrade, Nigeria National Bureau of Statistics retail food-price data, and World Bank WDI. The repository documentation notes that different datasets cover different periods and that the source data are not bundled in the public repository.

Complete

Data Preparation

The analytical workflow includes source auditing, file-structure checks, standardisation of column names and state names, handling missing values and duplicates, outlier flagging, crop identifier harmonisation, date/year normalisation, and staging of cleaned outputs as CSV files before loading into SQLite. The methodology describes this as a standardised pipeline supporting 17 analytical queries and a seven-page Power BI dashboard.

Complete

Methodology

The project follows a documented end-to-end pipeline: raw data, cleaning and standardisation, staging, SQLite database, analytical SQL, Power BI dashboard, and executive reporting. The composite food-security score is a 0–100 project-specific analytical construct using three pillars: availability (40%), self-sufficiency (30%), and welfare (30%). It is explicitly not an official Nigerian or international food-security index.

Complete

Analysis

The repository contains 17 analytical SQL queries covering household crop adoption, state and regional production, national production trends, crop yield trajectories, food supply versus minimum requirements, import dependency, trade sourcing, food prices and inflation, yield gaps, input-use intensity, and composite food-security scoring. The Power BI dashboard is organised into seven pages: National Overview, Regional Production Analysis, Crop Analysis, Import Dependence, Trade Sourcing, Food Prices & Access, and Food Security Summary.

Complete

Results

The project reports a composite food-security score average of 77.2/100 across 2010–2023, noting a declining trend after 2019. National crop production rose by approximately 45%, from 160 million tonnes in 2010 to 232 million tonnes in 2022. Caloric supply reached 2,520 kcal/capita/day in 2022, while undernourishment rose from 10.4% in 2010 to 18.7% in 2022, with the latest WDI estimate used for 2023 at 19.9%. Maize was the leading crop by household adoption, with more than 69,000 survey households, and Kano was the leading state in survey harvest volume at approximately 22 million kg. The food import bill reached USD 9.57 billion in 2022.

Interpretation

The project interprets the evidence as a food-security paradox: aggregate production and caloric availability increased while undernourishment worsened. This points to the importance of economic access, prices, trade exposure, distribution, and welfare conditions rather than production alone. The project explicitly distinguishes this descriptive interpretation from causal inference.

Complete

Limitations

The project documents several important limitations: LSMS evidence is from the 2018–2019 wave and should not be treated as an annual production time series; different datasets cover different periods; UN Comtrade net_weight_kg was zero for all 3,997 Nigerian import records examined, so trade value was used instead; CPI base-year changes limit direct comparison of absolute index levels across distant periods; and the composite score is a project-specific analytic construct, not an official benchmark or index. The analysis is primarily descriptive and should not automatically be interpreted as causal evidence.

Complete

Recommendations

The project identifies areas for further policy and analytical attention, including market infrastructure, post-harvest loss reduction, targeted social protection, and import substitution for highly import-dependent commodities such as wheat and sugar. These are policy implications drawn from the combined project evidence, not causal estimates.

Lessons Learned

The project demonstrates that a high-production food system can still face welfare challenges if access, prices, trade exposure, and distribution are weak. It also reinforces the value of rigorous data auditing: trade weight and CPI methodological issues materially affect interpretation, and source-period differences require careful analysis rather than direct aggregation.

Complete

Next Steps

The repository points to further analytical and policy attention around market infrastructure, post-harvest loss reduction, targeted social protection, and strategic import substitution for commodities with high import dependence, particularly wheat and sugar. The project also recognises the need for source validation and careful interpretation of data with different temporal coverage and methodological constraints.
